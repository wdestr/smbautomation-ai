01 / FOUNDATIONS

The one-sentence brief.

A seasoned operator built a product that actually works, documented it without bullshit, and invited you to test it before buying anything. Every design decision answers to that sentence.

Principle 01

Specifics over superlatives

"Answers in under 1 second" beats "lightning-fast." Numbers, cities, and named industries do the persuading.

Principle 02

One action per viewport

Amber appears once per screen, on the single thing we want clicked. It never competes with itself.

Principle 03

Proof, not promises

The product demos itself — a live transcript, a real number to call, a calculator that shows your number. Show, don't tell.

06 / MOTION

Restrained. Eased. Purposeful.

Fades and subtle positional eases, never bounces or springs. Motion proves the product is alive — a typing transcript, ticking counters — without ever slowing the page. Performance is conversion.

07 / VOICE

An operator talking to operators.

Short sentences. Real takes. Zero corporate filter. The buyer's money is always the subject — frame everything in revenue saved or captured.

Do

  • Name the buyer's industry: "Built for HVAC owners."
  • Lead with the outcome, then the feature.
  • Use specific numbers, cities, and timelines.
  • "A receptionist that never sleeps, never calls in sick."
  • Say what the AI actually does next.

Don't

  • Hype words: "revolutionary," "seamless," "game-changing."
  • "AI-powered" with no verb after it.
  • Feature lists above the fold.
  • "Contact us for pricing" on anything but enterprise.
  • Borrowed Fortune-500 credibility. Match the buyer.
